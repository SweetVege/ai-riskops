## 30-Second Pitch

AI RiskOps addresses a growing enterprise problem: AI applications are being shipped faster than governance teams can understand their risk posture. I designed and built a working platform that records model calls, detects AI-specific risk events, explains matched rules and evidence, and helps governance leaders, analysts, app owners, and platform admins view risk from the right scope.

The demo is deployed on Vercel with Neon Postgres and includes 5,000+ model calls, 620+ risk events, scoped APIs, GitHub Actions CI, and product surfaces for Overview, Risk Analytics, Risk Events, Call Logs, Applications, and Admin.

## 3-Minute Demo Route

### 1. Overview

Goal: show enterprise-level AI risk posture.

What to show:

- Model Calls Today, Risk Events, Blocked, and Average Event Risk Score.
- Risk Level Trend with High+Severe Rate overlay.
- Risk Category Distribution, Top Risky Applications, and Severe Event Snapshot.

Talk track:

Overview is designed for governance leaders and AI risk owners. It separates AI usage volume from detected risk events and highlights which applications and risk categories are driving the enterprise risk posture.

### 2. Risk Analytics

Goal: explain why risk changed.

What to show:

- Risk Event Rate, High+Severe Rate, Average Risk Score, Block Rate, Rule Hit Rate, and Log Coverage Score.
- Driver analysis by application, category, rule, environment, user role, and data type.
- LLM-style insight summaries and drill-down paths.

Talk track:

Risk Analytics turns dashboard movement into investigation direction. Analysts can identify the main contributors behind severe-risk increases and use the insight copy to explain findings in business language.

### 3. Risk Events

Goal: show evidence-based investigation.

What to show:

- Event list with risk level, system action, human review status, SLA, and owner.
- Matched Rules & Evidence.
- Linked Call Log.

Talk track:

Risk Events is not a full remediation workflow. It is an analyst workbench for discovering, explaining, and prioritizing AI application risks before downstream teams decide what remediation or governance action to take.

### 4. Call Logs

Goal: show traceability.

What to show:

- Model-call records, trace IDs, risk scores, actions, linked events, and masked content.

Talk track:

Call Logs are the audit backbone. Every risk event should remain traceable to the model call that produced it, while normal API responses avoid exposing raw sensitive content.

### 5. Applications

Goal: show app-level risk profiles and scoped visibility.

What to show:

- Application inventory, owner teams, onboarding state, field coverage, and app-level metrics.
- Switch to App Owner to show assigned-application scope.

Talk track:

Application-level views matter because governance teams care about the whole portfolio, while app owners need only the apps they manage.

### 6. Admin

Goal: show platform configuration.

What to show:

- Policy Center, Application Setup, credentials, ingestion audit, and User Access.

Talk track:

Admin separates sensitive platform controls from normal risk observation. Platform Admins can configure policies, onboard applications, manage ingestion credentials, and control access.

## Interview Narrative

I started this project from a product problem: enterprises are adopting LLM, RAG, Copilot, and Agent applications quickly, but traditional logs do not explain AI-specific risks such as prompt injection, data leakage, RAG contamination, unauthorized access, unsafe tool use, or risky model outputs.

I positioned AI RiskOps as an observability and risk operations layer rather than a full audit or remediation workflow. The product records model-call activity, detects risk events, explains the matched rules and evidence behind each event, and gives different users the right level of visibility.

The first version supports governance leaders through an executive risk overview, analysts through Risk Analytics and Risk Events, app owners through application-scoped views, and platform admins through policy, application setup, credential, ingestion audit, and access configuration.

Technically, I moved the project from frontend-only prototype to an API-backed online product using Next.js App Router, TypeScript, Prisma, Neon Postgres, GitHub Actions, and Vercel. I also built a deterministic demo dataset with 5,000+ model calls, 620+ risk events, and 5,000+ ingestion audit records so the analytics surfaces show realistic operating patterns rather than tiny mock tables.

## Product Decisions To Emphasize

- AI RiskOps records, measures, and explains AI application risk; it does not replace GRC, audit, ticketing, or remediation tools.
- User access is modeled around capability and data scope, not fixed job titles.
- App Owner users see only assigned applications and related data.
- Policy Center and Application Setup live under Admin because they affect detection logic, onboarding, credentials, and ingestion behavior.
- Human review status exists for risk understanding and signal quality, not as a full case-management workflow.
- False-positive rate should come from completed human review outcomes, not from system guesses.
